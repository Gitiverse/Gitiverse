# Hi, I'm Sulaiman 👋

I build things people can actually use. Sometimes that means machine learning. Often it's a simple program that works without Wi-Fi.

My school didn't offer computer science, so I taught myself through free online courses, starting on a laptop that shut down if you tugged the cable.

Right now I'm an intern at **SherlockIT**, an AI startup, working on document AI.

## What I've built

**[📋 LogiMind: offline medical logbook](https://github.com/Gitiverse/Logimind_v1)**  
A doctor in my family spent almost an hour every night copying patient cases into a logbook. I built an app she could use during rounds instead. It's fully offline because the Wi-Fi kept dropping and patient records shouldn't go online. She designs her own fields (checkboxes, dropdowns), and it saves her about an hour a day. I designed the database and every screen, and used AI coding tools to write the code.

**👕 Fashion-MNIST CNN** (learning project)  
While learning PyTorch, I trained a CNN that recognizes clothing 88.1% of the time. The part that stuck with me is where it fails: it mixed up shirts and T-shirts 176 times, and I want to know why.

**🌱 Smart irrigation app** (Reboot the Earth hackathon)  
I led a team of five on an app that tells farmers what their soil needs. It speaks instructions aloud for farmers with limited literacy, runs a small model locally on low-powered devices, and uses a Raspberry Pi with an antenna to capture radio weather broadcasts when the internet fails.

**📑 AI contract-management app** (AIX Summit)  
A working Streamlit app for managing contracts with AI. I built it after learning the hard way that judges trust what they can touch, not a PowerPoint.

## How I think

Before I build anything, I ask: **does this problem even need AI?** Plain programming is cheaper, more predictable, and works offline. Sometimes AI wins the case. Usually, a few if-statements would do.
